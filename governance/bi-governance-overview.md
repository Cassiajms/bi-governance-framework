# BI Governance Overview

Business Intelligence governance is the foundation that ensures analytics initiatives remain reliable, scalable and aligned with business needs over time.

In many organisations, BI challenges do not stem from a lack of data or tools, but from unclear ownership, inconsistent definitions and unmanaged growth of reports and models.

This document outlines a practical, experience-based approach to BI governance.

---

## What BI Governance Is (and Is Not)

BI governance is about **clarity, accountability and sustainability**.

It is:
- Clear ownership of data, reports and KPIs
- Defined rules for creating, changing and retiring reports
- Shared understanding of business definitions
- Controls that protect data quality and trust

It is **not**:
- Bureaucracy for its own sake
- Tool-specific configurations
- A one-time documentation exercise

---

## Core Governance Principles

### 1. Ownership Over Access
Every dataset, report and KPI must have a clearly defined owner.
Access without ownership leads to uncontrolled changes and loss of trust.

### 2. One Definition, Many Views
KPIs and metrics must be defined once and reused consistently.
Different views are acceptable; different definitions are not.

### 3. Lifecycle Thinking
Reports should be treated as products with a lifecycle:
creation, maintenance, review and retirement.

### 4. Business Before Tools
Governance decisions are driven by business logic and usage, not by technical convenience.

### 5. Documentation as an Asset
Documentation is not optional.
It enables continuity when teams, tools or vendors change.

---

## Typical Governance Gaps Observed in BI Environments

- Multiple reports answering the same question differently
- No clarity on who owns or validates KPIs
- Reports created for one-off requests that become permanent
- Knowledge concentrated in individuals rather than documented
- Technical teams disconnected from business decision-makers

---

## Governance Scope

A BI governance framework typically covers:
- Report inventory and classification
- KPI definition and validation
- Data quality controls
- Ownership and accountability models
- Change and release processes
- Documentation standards

This framework is designed to be **tool-agnostic** and adaptable to:
- SQL-based platforms
- On-prem, cloud or hybrid architectures
- Different BI tools and analytics stacks

---

## Outcome of Effective BI Governance

When governance is implemented correctly, organisations gain:
- Increased trust in data and reporting
- Faster decision-making
- Reduced rework and technical debt
- Easier onboarding of new team members
- Long-term sustainability of BI initiatives
